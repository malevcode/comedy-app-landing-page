FAQ

ANSWERS TO THINGS COMEDIANS ACTUALLY ASK

Recording

Why does the app tell me to turn on Airplane Mode?

Open mic venues — especially basements — have notoriously bad cell signal. A call, notification, or dropped signal mid-set can corrupt your recording or cause it to cut out entirely. Airplane Mode removes all of that risk. The app reminds you every single time, no "don't show again" option, because it's that important.

Once the set is done and you've stopped recording, turn Airplane Mode off and the app uploads and processes automatically.

What if I forget to turn Airplane Mode back on and lose my connection mid-upload?

The app has an offline queue. If you're not connected when a set finishes recording, it saves the audio locally and queues it for processing. As soon as you have a connection, it drains the queue automatically — you don't have to do anything.

How do I stop the recording?

Hold the "HOLD TO STOP" button for 2.5 seconds. You'll feel a haptic at the start and a stronger one when it confirms. This is intentional — it prevents accidentally stopping your set with a tap.

How long can I record?

There's no hard limit in the app. The practical limit is AssemblyAI's transcription API (handles files up to several hours) and Supabase Storage (generous on the free tier). A typical open mic set (5–7 minutes) or feature spot (20 minutes) is well within range.

Analysis

How does Comediq know when I got a laugh?

It uses two signals: pause analysis and Claude's interpretation.

During transcription, AssemblyAI returns word-level timestamps. Gaps longer than 800ms between words are flagged as potential laugh moments — the longer the gap, the bigger the laugh proxy score. Claude then reads the full transcript and separately evaluates which bits likely landed based on phrasing, structure, and context.

Neither signal is perfect. The laugh detection is a proxy — it doesn't actually hear the audience. If you record in a noisy room and speak over the laughs, the pauses won't be there. But across multiple sets, the patterns become meaningful.

What does the bit score mean?

Each bit gets a score from 1–10 based on Claude's analysis of the transcript. It considers structure (setup/punchline clarity), technique (callbacks, misdirection, rule of threes, crowd work), laugh proxy signals, and overall delivery pattern. 7+ is green, 5–6 is blue, below 5 is red.

The score reflects one performance. A bit that consistently scores 7+ across multiple sets is genuinely working. A bit that scores 4 once might just have been a bad night.

What are the tag colors I see in the transcript?

When TAGS are turned on, Claude annotates your transcript with color-coded markers:

FUNNY moments that likely got a laugh or landed well
FLUFF sections that didn't add much — filler, throat-clearing, tangents
ON STAGE things you said on stage that differ from what you'd written

Turn tags off if you just want to read your transcript clean.

Does Comediq record what the audience says?

It records whatever your phone's microphone picks up. Audience reactions, laughter, crowd work responses — if your phone can hear it, it gets transcribed. This is actually useful for reviewing crowd work exchanges. All of that audio is subject to the same privacy handling described in our Privacy Policy.

You are responsible for complying with any applicable recording consent laws in your jurisdiction when recording in venues.

Joke CRM

What is a "bit identity"?

A bit identity is the canonical record for a joke — the single source of truth that tracks every time you've performed that bit across different sets. When you record a set, Comediq uses fuzzy name matching to figure out which detected bits correspond to which joke identities. That way, you can see "this bit has been performed 8 times, with an average score of 6.4" rather than 8 disconnected data points.

Why is my new bit in Archive instead of Active?

Bits detected from a recording start in Archive with a status of "premise." This is intentional — recordings pick up a lot of premises, crowd work fragments, and half-formed ideas that aren't active works-in-progress yet. Active should only contain what you're consciously developing right now.

Promote a bit to Active by tapping it and changing the status to "being written," "has punchline," or "performance ready."

I have duplicate jokes with slightly different names. How do I fix that?

Use the Merge feature in the Archive section. Tap Merge, select which identity to keep (the "winner"), tap the duplicate you want to absorb, and confirm. All performances from the duplicate get reassigned to the keeper, and the duplicate is deleted.

The fuzzy matching threshold (75% similarity) catches most duplicates automatically, but bits with meaningfully different names will slip through and need manual merging.

What does "auto-retire" mean?

If you haven't performed a bit in 30 days, Comediq automatically moves it to "retired" status in your Archive. It doesn't get deleted — the history and scores are all still there. It just gets out of your Active list so you're not looking at a joke you've dropped.

You can un-retire it any time by changing the status back.

Data & Privacy

Where is my audio stored?

Audio files are uploaded to Supabase Storage, a cloud storage service. Files are stored in a private bucket — they're not publicly accessible via URL without authentication. See our Privacy Policy for full details.

Does Comediq read my jokes?

Yes — that's the product. Your transcript is sent to Anthropic's Claude API for analysis. Claude reads the full transcript, identifies bits, scores them, and returns structured data. Anthropic's API processes your data under their privacy policy (anthropic.com/privacy). We do not use your jokes to train any AI models.

Can I delete my data?

Yes. Individual sets can be deleted from within the app (swipe or long-press). If you want everything deleted — all sets, bits, recordings, and notes — email adam@comediq.us and we'll wipe it within 7 days.
